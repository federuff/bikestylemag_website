Anyone who rides a bike to work every day knows the problem well: most cycling accessories
are designed for sport, not for everyday life. Overly technical bags, bulky locks, lights
that look like they belong on a construction site.

In recent years, several brands have started tackling the problem from the opposite
direction, starting from urban design rather than sports design: waterproof bags smart
enough for a meeting, locks that integrate into the frame without ruining its lines, minimal
lighting systems that only draw attention when needed.

## Commuting as a design category in its own right

It's a product category gaining its own identity: no longer just "bike accessories", but
objects designed to accompany anyone who lives city life on two wheels every day, with the
same aesthetic care you'd give a backpack or a wallet.

The implicit message is clear: riding to work shouldn't mean giving up on style, or showing
up at the office dressed for a race.
