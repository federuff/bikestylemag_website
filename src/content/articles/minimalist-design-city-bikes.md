There's a trend running through the city bike world lately: a search for the essential.
Fewer levers, fewer visible cables, fewer decorations on the frame. In their place, a single
matte colour, clean geometry, and an almost obsessive attention to details you only notice up
close — the frame weld, the shape of the saddle, the typeface etched into the down tube.

![Minimal frame in a single matte colour](/images/articles/city-bike-design-minimale-dettaglio.jpg)

## Less is more, even on two wheels

This approach speaks the language of product design directly: few elements, all of them
necessary, none superfluous. It's no surprise that several industrial design studios,
previously strangers to the cycling world, have started signing collaborations with bicycle
manufacturers.

The result is a bicycle built to be looked at almost as much as it's built to be ridden: an
object as at home leaning against a wall at home as it is locked to a rack in the city
centre.
